## Opportunity A1 — Keep clock talk and routine talk separate

**What is being enriched or supported?**  
Clear separation between *What time is it? / It’s … o’clock* (`bep2_lang_u6_001`) and *When do/does…? / … at [time]* (`bep2_lang_u6_002`, `bep2_lang_u6_003`).

**Why might students need it?**  
Both use clock times; younger learners may blend the question shapes. This is a teaching consideration inferred from the coexistence of two frames—not a claim that students have failed.

**Evidence:**  
- **Textbook:** Two distinct language rows and components (clock song/vocab vs Language in Action).  
- **Teacher judgment:** Separation reduces mixed models during pair work.

**Smallest practical teacher action:**  
30–60 seconds before Act 9–12: show one clock question and one routine question side by side; say which lesson goal is today’s (routines).

**Priority class:** Optional reinforcement (conditional — recommend if students mix the two question types).

**Unnecessary if…**  
Students already ask and answer *When do/does…?* without defaulting to *What time is it?*

## Opportunity A2 — Pair *do/does* in the question with *-s* in the answer

**What is being enriched or supported?**  
Productive control of the full exchange: helper choice (*do/does*) plus third-person *-s* in the answer (*gets up*, *starts*), using book vocabulary only.

**Why might students need it?**  
Act 10 isolates *do/does* in writing; model answers show *-s*, but the book does not always force both halves of the exchange in one short retrieval cycle. Learners may get one half right and drop the other. No performance data claimed.

**Evidence:**  
- **Textbook:** Act 10 instruction (“write *do* or *does*”); model answers with *-s* (`bep2_lang_u6_002`, Act 12 frame).  
- **Teacher judgment:** Short paired drill is a common way to bind question form to answer form.

**Smallest practical teacher action:**  
2–3 minutes after Act 10: teacher prompts three picture cues; students produce full Q→A (*When does she get up? She gets up at 7:00.*) with no new vocabulary.

**Priority class:** Optional reinforcement (conditional — recommend if written *do/does* is fine but spoken answers drop *-s*, or the reverse).

**Unnecessary if…**  
Students already produce accurate full exchanges in Act 12-style pair work.

## Opportunity A3 — Make *at* audible when modelling time answers

**What is being enriched or supported?**  
Consistent use of *at* before clock times in routine answers (*at 6:00*), as already shown in every core model.

**Why might students need it?**  
Phase 1 shows *at* in all focus models, but there is no activity that asks students to choose or fill *at* the way Act 10 asks for *do/does*. Learners may echo the verb and time and quietly omit the preposition. Still a consideration—not diagnosed failure.

**Evidence:**  
- **Textbook:** *at* present in `bep2_lang_u6_002`–`004` answers; no dedicated *at* practice item found in the focus activities.  
- **Teacher judgment:** A brief highlight can close a quiet practice gap without new grammar lectures.

**Smallest practical teacher action:**  
While modelling one Act 12 answer, tap or stress *at*; if a student says a bare time, recast once with *at* and have the class repeat that one sentence.

**Priority class:** Optional reinforcement (conditional — recommend if bare times appear). **Not** essential: the book does model *at* repeatedly; absence of a fill-in item is a mild practice skew, not missing curriculum content.

**Unnecessary if…**  
Students already include *at* in spoken and written routine answers.
