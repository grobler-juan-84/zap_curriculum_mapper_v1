## Main language target

Same textbook core as Phase 1 shows: **routine-time questions and answers**.

Phase 2 labels the focus structure **STR-02** (*Asking and Talking About Daily Routine Times*), role `main_target`, pattern:

*When do/does [subject] [base verb]? / [Subject] [verb(-s/-es)] at [time].*

Examples remain book-evidenced: *When does he get up? He gets up at 6:00.* / *When do you go to bed? I go to bed at 8:00.* (`bep2_lang_u6_002`, `bep2_lang_u6_003`).

**Nearby but distinct (keep separate in teaching):**

- **STR-01** — *What time is it? / It’s … o’clock* (supports times; different question).
- **STR-03** — *start/finish* schedule questions (`bep2_lang_u6_004`).
- **STR-04** — broader Wh- + *be/do/does* practice (`bep2_lang_u6_005`); useful later in the unit, not the first routine-time goal.

## Foundational knowledge evident from the source

Phase 2 names the mechanics that Phase 1 shows in examples and Act 10, without inventing new textbook content:

| ID | Skill (V3.1) | Teaching status | Why it matters for this focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **FS-02** | Choosing *do* vs *does* | explicit | Required in *When do/does…?* questions |
| **FS-01** | Third-person *-s/-es* | explicit; frequent-reinforcement candidate | Required in *He/She …s at …* answers |
| **FS-03** | Preposition *at* before clock times | **implicit** in the book | Present in every core answer; easy to under-teach because it is not titled as a grammar point |
| **FS-04** | Clock face ↔ number + *o’clock* | explicit | Prerequisite support for time answers |

**Cross-unit (only if useful for this focus):**

- **CON-02** (related_knowledge): Third-person *-s* in U6 routines also appears in U8 habitat facts (*lives* / *live*). Reminds teachers the inflection is a **general present-simple rule**, not a “daily routines only” trick—without claiming U8 must be taught first.
- **CON-01** (related_knowledge): U1 and U6 both practise Wh- + auxiliary/linking-verb order. For this focus, use lightly: *When* + *do/does* is the same “question word + helping verb before the subject” habit students met with other Wh- frames—not a reason to re-teach all of Unit 1.

Connections CON-05–08 (phonics/commands) and CON-03/04 are omitted here as lower relevance to the stated focus.

## Possible student difficulties (teaching considerations — not textbook facts)

Still teaching considerations, now tagged more precisely:

1. **FS-02 / FS-01 together:** Wrong helper (*do/does*) often pairs with missing *-s* in the answer.
2. **FS-03 (*at*):** Implicit in the book—students may produce bare times.
3. **STR-01 vs STR-02 confusion:** *What time is it?* vs *When do you…?*
4. **CON-02 caution:** Do not assume students transfer *-s* from U6 to U8 (or the reverse) automatically; treat transfer as a teaching opportunity, not evidence of prior mastery.

Same Phase 1 audio gaps apply (Tracks 122 / 133). I Can sentences remain unextracted (`bep2_p105` note only).

## Practical teaching considerations

1. Lead with **STR-02** (routines + *at + time*); use **STR-01** as a short warm-up, not the main talk.
2. Make **FS-02** and **FS-01** visible in the same practice cycle (question form + answer form)—matching what Act 9–10 already demand.
3. Say the word ***at*** aloud when modelling answers; because FS-03 is implicit, a 30-second highlight can prevent a quiet gap.
4. If students already stumble on Wh- + helper order, a brief pointer to the shared pattern with earlier Wh- questions (**CON-01**) may help—without opening a full Unit 1 review.
5. Optionally preview that the same *-s* rule will matter again in later factual sentences (**CON-02** / U8)—one sentence of awareness is enough.
6. Keep CLIL, culture, values, and phonics as supporting strands unless today’s lesson is about those pages.
