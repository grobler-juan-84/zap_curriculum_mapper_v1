## Paste block (start)

```text
Create a SHORT slide lesson to SUPPORT Big English 2 SB Unit 8 pages 128–129 ONLY.
Do NOT teach later Unit 8 pages. Do NOT replace the textbook.
Do NOT include adjective order, habitats posters, "Can it/they…? Yes/No" short-answer drills, "They're so…", or phonics — those come later in the unit.
Age-appropriate English. Pictures for animals. Few words per slide. No filler.

════════════════════════════════════
A. CURRICULUM FLOOR (REQUIRED) — pp. 128–129
════════════════════════════════════

Page 128 — Vocabulary "Wild Animals":
Animals to learn / say:
giraffe, hippo, kangaroo, cheetah, polar bear, zebra, parrot, monkey, peacock, elephant, crocodile, snake

Textbook activities to prepare for:
- Listen, look, and say.
- Listen, find, and say.
- Play a game. (keep simple — teacher can run their own game)

Page 129 — Song "To the Zoo!" + talk:
Song introduces ability with can / can't (examples from the song):
- A kangaroo can jump. / A monkey can jump, too.
- Crocodiles can chase and swim.
- A giraffe can't fly… / An elephant can't climb trees. / Fish can't run… / hippos can't fly…
Word from the page: chase

Language frame on this page (required model):
Do you like cheetahs? → Yes, I do! Cheetahs can run.

Textbook activities to prepare for:
- Listen and sing. Then look at the animal pictures and find.
- Listen and say true or false.
- Look at the animals. Ask and answer using:
  Do you like [animals]? Yes, I do! [Animals] can [action].

Communicative aim for THESE pages:
Students can name the wild animals and begin to say what animals can / can't do, using preference as a friendly lead-in — ready for the book tasks on 128–129.

════════════════════════════════════
B. TEACHING PROGRESSION (REQUIRED)
════════════════════════════════════

1) Animal pictures — hear / see / say the 12 names (page 128).
2) Quick recognition game (point / find / say) — still page 128.
3) Song support — show 2–3 clear can / can't lines with pictures (jump, swim, fly, climb, chase, run). Students listen and notice; do not turn this into a full grammar lecture.
4) Model the page-129 frame once or twice:
   Do you like cheetahs? → Yes, I do! Cheetahs can run.
5) Guided practice with other unit animals + simple verbs from the song/page.
6) Reduced support → pair ask/answer (same frame).
7) Stop. Leave the textbook activities for the teacher and book.

Always prefer: Model → guided practice → student speaking.
Recycle only these animals. Do not add new animals.

Keep separate:
- "Do you like …?" = feeling
- "… can / can't …" = what it does

════════════════════════════════════
C. PRIOR LEARNING (OPTIONAL — USE LIGHTLY)
════════════════════════════════════

Only if helpful — do NOT assume mastery; do NOT teach new continuous grammar:
- If students know action talk ("It's jumping"), one quick bridge: It's jumping. → It can jump. Then stay on can/can't for the song/page.
- Do NOT teach "Can I help you?" here (that is offering help, not animal ability).

════════════════════════════════════
D. OPTIONAL ENRICHMENT (OPTIONAL — BLANK IS OK)
════════════════════════════════════

Teachers may skip these and still complete pp. 128–129.

1) One two-beat model card: Do you like cheetahs? → Cheetahs can run. (feeling vs ability)
2) Extra naming turns with the same 12 animals (see / say / find) if the class needs more floor support.

Do NOT add: Can it/they short-answer chains; full adjective order; habitats; "They're so…"; past could; new animal lists.

════════════════════════════════════
E. GENERATION RULES
════════════════════════════════════

- Short student sentences. No teaching jargon on slides.
- A clear picture for every animal you show.
- One idea per slide. No duplicate filler slides.
- Prioritize naming + the like → can frame + a little can/can't from the song.
- Leave space for the teacher’s own game / true-false / sing-along.
- Success = students are ready for the book activities on pages 128–129.
```
